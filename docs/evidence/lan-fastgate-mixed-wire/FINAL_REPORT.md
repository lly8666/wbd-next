## Hard limitations and decision

**The trusted TLS app label is an offline test oracle, NOT a general TLS classifier.** The O(1) gate validates the test-supplied label by original TCP port/seq/length and outer TLS-looking header; this does not give the label provenance. TLS1.3 encrypted handshake, application and alert can each have outer 17 03 03. Neither packet size nor that header proves inner TLS application data. Robust live flow reconstruction, HRR, fragmentation and authenticated receiver mode admission would need separate engineering and testing. Unknown labels always AES in this experiment. A real receiver that trusts an attacker-supplied XOR mode can be downgraded; the lab receiver instead consults the test's expected mode.

**Public-PN XOR is entirely reversible and unauthenticated.** Even when sensitive TLS1.3 ClientHello contents were encrypted in the controlled capture, XOR paths disclose original inner IP/TCP headers and inner encrypted TLS application bytes after trivial reversal, permit tampering and replay, and are distinguishable from real TLS1.3 ciphertext.

The observation is a convincing isolated positive test for **fallback rule + wire appearance + record-level CPU**, NOT proof of real WBD tunnel security, real traffic classification, end-to-end Go→Rust→TUN, application throughput, CPU whole process, or p99. Scoped stop: keep isolated and do not merge into production.
