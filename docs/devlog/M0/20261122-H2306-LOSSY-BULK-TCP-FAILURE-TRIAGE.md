## Concrete Rust source: extra lossy queue on real ARM Linux outbound-to-client TCP ACK path

Archived actual Rust binary source `97db898f3a1fbebcdeea7f2a1b9d9c3dfcd32573` matches `crates/wbd-engine/src/v4_embedded_go.rs`: `SHARED_TUN_PENDING_PER_ROUTE=8` packets, `SHARED_TUN_PENDING_BYTES_PER_ROUTE=64KiB`, and `SHARED_TUN_PENDING_MAX_AGE=100ms`. Real `enqueue_shared_tun` uses bounded `try_send` to M1 owner and increments `d.overflow` on per-route queue full. This is **real data/ACK loss beyond external netem**; it may deepen TCP fast-recovery/RTO. Widening this queue without evidence adds latency/HOL and is **not authorized** as fix. The original binary already supports source-locked **read-only** `WBD_SHARED_TUN_ROUTE_DIAG=1` that records `tcp_new_dropped`, `udp_new_dropped`, `downstream_full`, and pending bytes at owner Stop.

A classical single-connection loss-controlled TCP throughput sanity-check `~MSS/(RTT*sqrt(loss))` at MSS ~1169B, RTT400ms, loss1/5% predicts severe loss scaling, consistent with direction and scale of relay byte-counter observation. This is **not** a proof of Reno or BBR/CUBIC mode and is **not** an actual receiver goodput. Windows actual sending stage bottleneck is **draining ACKed bytes after FIN**, not a blocked userspace send loop.

## Additional qualified product receipts, beyond initial timeouts

- **H2306 1% at 2MiB is not a universal permanent deadlock.** [Original group-a client result](https://github.com/lly8666/wbd-next/actions/runs/44987867357/job/137102477769) submitted `10,059,776 / 10,829,824 / 11,730,944 / 9,158,656` local bytes for flows 0–3 during 25s supply. **Three of four** flows received genuine ARM 8-byte exact-byte count receipts at `188.250s / 189.063s / 193.235s`, while flow0 was still waiting at the finite test join deadline. `finished_bulk_flows=3`, `echo_received=160`, `errors=[]` but overall A correctly **FAIL / INCOMPLETE**. This distinguishes normal eventual recovery under severe loss from a hard protocol deadlock; it is also direct evidence that fixed 180s startup/receipt timing is too tight for a slow bulk tail but **cannot be relaxed to pretend 400Mbps was achieved**. Different independent earlier 1%-loss runner A still had 0/4 done by 205.7s; do not pick only the favorable sample.
- **H2306 5% at 2MiB** original both independent group [a](https://github.com/lly8666/wbd-next/actions/runs/44987375681/job/137100910587) and [b](https://github.com/lly8666/wbd-next/actions/runs/44987375681/job/137100910563) each reported `echo_received=160`, `finished_bulk_flows=0`, and three workers explicitly `error_at_phase=await_arm_receipt` at ~205.5s after local 25s send supply. The fourth worker in each group was also at `await_arm_receipt` without completed receipt at snapshot time. Netem 5% both directions remains genuine and distinct from original Rust route-full ACK losses. No completed 5%-loss goodput may be claimed.
- Combined with H2304 `A8/16MiB FAIL`, this rules out large socket buffer alone as root. Window affects amount of enqueued but unconfirmed byte supply; at 5% even 2MiB + 25s supplies ~9MiB per flow and is much too slow for the finite drain deadline. At 1% some flows eventually finish at ~190s, not all. The **primary measured failure mechanism is dramatically reduced kernel-TCP bulk throughput and long-tail close drain**, with additional original ARM per-route shared-TUN queue drops as a measurable amplifier, not original TLS or Npcap startup.
- The **latest causal question** is whether fixed FEC2 in original Windows Rust sender and ARM Rust receiver protects the real random-loss substrate enough for complete TCP bulk delivery without huge buffers.
